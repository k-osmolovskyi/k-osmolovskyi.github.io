
Comparative Clarifications and Distinctions for the GTCS

| Framework / family | What it explains well | What it typically leaves collapsed or implicit for the present target | GTCS distinction / contribution | Why existing terminology is insufficient here |
|-------------------|----------------------|---------------------------------------------------------------------|--------------------------------|---------------------------------------------|
| **Predictive processing / active inference** | Expectation-guided regulation, prediction error minimization, hierarchical inference, adaptive updating under uncertainty. | Often centers error minimization or inferential updating without explicitly separating processing within an architecture from admissibility of changing the architecture itself; may also leave overload-retention and pre-symbolic admissibility as distinct layers underspecified. | GTCS separates geometric coherence, discrepancy accessibility, representation, and structural updating. It also formalizes overload memory as an additional state dimension and treats admissibility of structural updating as a distinct problem. | "Prediction error" or "belief updating" is not sufficient when the target is admissibility of structural reorganization rather than adaptive update within a standing inferential architecture. |
| **Control theory / cybernetic regulation** | Feedback, stability, error correction, regulation relative to targets, control under perturbation. | Often treats regulation functionally without explicitly formalizing historically constituted invariants, overload-retention, or restricted accessibility of structural deviation. | GTCS preserves a stronger distinction between global structural condition and regulatorily accessible signal, and between ordinary regulation and admissibility of structural updating. | "Control" or "feedback" is too broad if the target is to distinguish access, representation, retained overload, and architecture-level transformation. |
| **Dynamical systems approaches** | Attractors, trajectories, phase-space behavior, bifurcation, metastability, nonlinear evolution. | Often provides rich dynamics but may leave the architectural meaning of state dimensions, admissible domains, and update constraints underspecified. | GTCS uses geometric and dynamical tools but binds them to admissibility, overload history, invariant structure, and level transition criteria. | General dynamical vocabulary is not enough when one must distinguish geometric coherence, admissibility of discrepancy, and admissibility of structural updating. |
| **Connectionist / learning-based adaptive models** | Learning from data, distributed adjustment, plastic change, optimization over repeated exposure. | Often treats structural change and weight adaptation within one broad learning notion; may leave architecture-preserving and architecture-changing processes insufficiently separated. | GTCS explicitly distinguishes local adaptation, learning-like change, and admissible structural updating. | "Learning" is too coarse when the theoretical target is the boundary between processing and structural reorganization. |
| **Cognitive dissonance / conflict frameworks** | Tension, contradiction, ambivalence, motivation to reduce inconsistency, conflict-like psychological burden. | Typically focuses on psychologically salient inconsistency without formalizing invariant structure, admissible geometry, overload memory, or access/representation separation. | GTCS defines conflict structurally, links it to invariant interaction architecture, and embeds it in a broader regulatory geometry. | "Dissonance" is too narrow and too psychologized if the target is structural incompatibility across general cognitive architectures. |
| **Stress-load / resilience / allostatic models** | Accumulated burden, adaptation under load, wear-and-tear, resilience, threshold effects. | Often captures load accumulation well but may not distinguish overload memory as a formal regulatory state variable deforming admissibility geometry; may also leave structural updating and accessibility layers implicit. | GTCS formalizes overload as excess beyond compensability, overload memory as a retained state component, and admissibility as history-sensitive through (T, L) geometry. | Generic "stress" or "load" terminology is not enough when the retained variable itself must be formalized as part of admissibility. |
| **Global workspace / access-consciousness style frameworks** | Broadcast, access, reportability, availability for coordinated processing. | Often focuses on availability or accessibility without separating pre-symbolic admissibility, representational construction, and geometric structural condition. | GTCS introduces a prior filtering layer: pre-symbolic admissibility, then admissible discrepancy domain, then representation. | "Access" is too coarse when the target is to distinguish pre-representational gating from later accessible representation. |
| **Representationalist internal-state models** | Internal variables, latent state estimation, internal monitoring, proxy-based regulation. | Often captures internal proxies well but may not preserve the distinction between representation and the full global structure represented. | GTCS explicitly states that coherence representation is not a reconstruction of full geometric coherence and may preserve order without preserving metric structure. | "Internal representation" alone is insufficient when non-reconstructive, order-preserving proxy status is the central theoretical point. |
| **Identity / self-model frameworks** | Continuity of self, self-description, self-representation, self-model maintenance, autobiographical persistence. | Often begins with self-related representation or narrative continuity rather than deriving persistence from bounded regulatory organization. | GTCS treats identity as a regulatory attractor and low-overload persistence structure, not as a primitive self-representational object. | "Self-model" or "identity narrative" is too narrow when the target is identity as an architectural consequence of regulatory dynamics. |
| **Multi-agent coordination / alignment models** | Coordination, strategic interaction, alignment conditions, compatibility of policies or behaviors. | Often addresses coordination externally or behaviorally without distinguishing admissible discrepancy structure, restricted accessibility, and ordinal transfer of internal regulatory variables. | GTCS allows inter-system comparability without shared metric geometry and defines inter-system conflict over admissible discrepancy domains. | "Alignment" is insufficient when the target is to distinguish order transfer from conflict defined at the level of admissible discrepancy structure. |
| **Complex systems / self-organization frameworks** | Emergence, macro-patterns, distributed order, self-organization, phase transitions. | Often explains spontaneous large-scale order well but may leave admissibility, retained overload, invariant architecture, and regulated accessibility too implicit. | GTCS can absorb self-organizing insights while adding explicit admissibility layers, historical retention, and architecture-sensitive update conditions. | General self-organization vocabulary is too weak when one needs explicit operators for admissibility, accessibility, and structural transformation. |
| **Formal abstraction / compression / concept-formation frameworks** | Abstraction, model reduction, compression, representation economy, category formation. | Often treats compression as simplification or summarization without distinguishing stabilizing compression from mere reduction, or level-preserving from level-forming transformation. | GTCS distinguishes compression from simplification and links compression to admissible transformation, invariant generation, and possible level transition. | "Compression" in the general sense is insufficient when the key issue is admissible structural transformation with geometric consequences. |
| **Phenomenology / introspection-oriented explanatory styles** | Lived structure, manifestation, felt organization, experiential articulation. | May richly describe manifestation but often does not formalize the architecture producing it or distinguish manifestation from accessibility and representation. | GTCS treats manifestation as downstream of architecture, access, and representation rather than as a primitive explanatory layer. | Manifestational language alone is insufficient when the target is the prior architecture that generates the manifest layer. |

---

**Why new terminology is introduced**

GTCS does not introduce new terminology merely to rename familiar phenomena. New
terms are used when one or more of the following applies:
- an existing term covers multiple distinct architectural layers at once;
- an existing term is tied to a local empirical domain rather than a general structural
role;
- an existing term presupposes a representational, semantic, or phenomenological reading
that is too narrow for the present target;
- an existing term does not distinguish between processing within an architecture and
admissibility of changing the architecture;
- an existing term leaves historical retention, accessibility constraints, or structural
non-equivalence implicit rather than formalized.
Typical examples include:
- learning is not sufficient if the target is admissibility of structural updating;
- error is not sufficient if the target is geometric coherence relative to an admissible
region;
- memory is not sufficient if the target is retained overload as a regulatory state
variable;
- implicit processing is not sufficient if the target is pre-symbolic admissibility as a
gating layer;
- self-model is not sufficient if the target is identity as regulatory attractor;
- disagreement is not sufficient if the target is inter-system conflict defined over
admissible discrepancy structure.

---

**Recurrent reduction errors to avoid**

The following reductions are especially likely to distort the explanatory target of GTCS:
- reducing structural updating to learning;
- reducing coherence to error, inconsistency, or subjective harmony;
- reducing overload memory to generic memory;
- reducing pre-symbolic admissibility to implicit representation;
- reducing representation to full structural transparency;
- reducing identity to narrative self-description;
- reducing inter-system conflict to disagreement alone;
- reducing compression to simplification or summarization;
- reducing accessibility to consciousness or reportability;
- reducing layered admissibility to a single undifferentiated threshold notion.
These reductions are not merely terminological. They change the unit of analysis and
often erase the distinctions that the present framework is built to preserve.

---

**Minimal comparative summary**

The comparative point of GTCS is not that alternative frameworks explain nothing. It
is that they often target adjacent phenomena under different primitives or with fewer
preserved layers. In the present framework, the main explanatory gains are:
- explicit separation between processing and structural updating;
- explicit separation between global structural condition and regulatorily accessible
signal;
- explicit treatment of overload memory as a retained state variable;
- explicit treatment of invariant structure as the source of admissible geometry;
- explicit treatment of compression as admissible structural transformation rather than
mere reduction;
- explicit introduction of pre-symbolic admissibility as a prior gating layer;
- explicit allowance for restricted accessibility of coherence;
- explicit support for cross-system comparison without requiring shared metric geometry.
These are the main reasons why some terminological novelty is retained rather than
absorbed into already familiar vocabulary.

---

**Short answer to the question**: *Why not just use
existing terminology?*

A concise answer may be stated as follows:
Existing terminology is reused whenever it preserves the relevant distinction. New terminology is introduced only where existing terms either collapse distinct architectural layers or presuppose a different explanatory target from the one pursued here. In this sense, terminological novelty in GTCS is not ornamental. It is a consequence of the decision to keep several explanatory layers explicitly separate.

---

**Closing note**

This document is intended as a comparative clarification note, not as a polemical rejection of existing theories. The aim is to make explicit why GTCS cannot be reduced without loss to more familiar vocabularies such as learning, error, access, stress, representation, identity, or alignment. Its claim is not that other frameworks fail everywhere, but that the present framework targets a specific architectural organization of distinctions that is not adequately preserved if those terms are reused without qualification.
