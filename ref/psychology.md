# Psychology

> GTCS does not replace psychological theory or clinical practice. It provides an architectural lens to distinguish structural constraints from content-level explanations, and trajectory-dependent overload from momentary distress.

| Problem / Open Question | GTCS Lens | What This Clarifies | Key References |
|-------------------------|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| **Cognitive rigidity / resistance to change** | Overload memory `L_t` + hysteresis in admissibility boundary `T_crit(L)` | Rigidity is not merely motivational or personality-based; it is a trajectory-dependent regulatory state where accumulated non-compensated burden shifts the admissibility threshold, making structural change regulatorily inadmissible even when information is processed. | [Rep. 2026-4](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19467913), [Rep. 2026-5](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19468033) |
| **Chronic stress and burnout** | Instantaneous overload `u_t` + accumulated overload memory `L_t` + compensability threshold `θ` | Burnout is not simply exhaustion or depletion; it is the architectural consequence of sustained non-compensated tension that accumulates into a persistent regulatory state, reducing effective tolerance for further pressure and biasing trajectories toward low-overload regions. | [Rep. 2026-4](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19467913), [Rep. 2026-6](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19476667) |
| **Decision-making under uncertainty** | Pre-symbolic admissibility `Π^ps_t` + admissible discrepancy domain `D_t` + coherence representation `Ĉ_t` | Decisions are not made over the full space of relevant information; they are constrained by which discrepancies are admitted into the regulatory domain, explaining why "obvious" alternatives may not enter deliberation even when cognitively available. | [Rep. 2026-11](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19499593), [Rep. 2026-9](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19488084) |
| **Trauma and persistent maladaptive patterns** | Identity-core as low-overload attractor + trajectory concentration + restricted accessibility | Maladaptive patterns persist not because of "faulty learning" alone, but because trajectories concentrate in low-overload regions that stabilize processing under bounded regulation; traumatic discrepancies may remain geometrically present but regulatorily inaccessible. | [Rep. 2026-6](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19476667), [Rep. 2026-12](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19508182) |
| **Self-concept stability and identity formation** | Identity as regulatory attractor + cost-separated low-overload regions + bounded regulation | Identity is not a narrative or self-model primitive; it is a structural consequence of trajectories concentrating in regions that minimize long-run regulatory pressure under invariant constraints, explaining persistence without metaphysical self. | [Rep. 2026-6](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19476667), [Rep. 2026-14](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19583268) |
| **Motivation and goal pursuit** | Coherence representation `Ĉ_t` + regime selection `ρ` + processing intensity `ι_t` | Motivation is not a scalar drive but a regulatory response to coherence-related significance: higher `Ĉ_t` induces higher intensity and regime transitions, explaining why goals feel compelling only when regulatorily accessible. | [Rep. 2026-9](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19488084), [Rep. 2026-5](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19468033) |
| **Emotional regulation and valence** | Valence asymmetry: negative = positional/directional, neutral = maintained burden, positive = directional only | Emotional valence is not symmetric: negative states may be static or dynamic, but positive manifestation is defined only through stabilization-directed movement, explaining why "relief" is more readily identifiable than "satisfaction". | [Rep. 2026-14](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19583268) |
| **Attention and selective processing** | Pre-symbolic admissibility `Π^ps_t` as structural gating filter + admissible discrepancy domain `D_t` | Attention is not merely resource allocation; it is the architectural consequence of which discrepancies are admitted into the regulatory domain prior to representation, explaining systematic blind spots without invoking capacity limits alone. | [Rep. 2026-11](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19499593), [Rep. 2026-12](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19508182) |
| **Memory and reconstructive processes** | Overload memory `L_t` as trajectory-dependent state + compression-induced invariant formation | Memory is not storage of past events but retention of structural constraints and overload traces; reconstructive processes arise from compression operations that replace distributed relations with compact invariants. | [Rep. 2026-4](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19467913), [Rep. 2026-8](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19481795) |
| **Interpersonal conflict and misalignment** | Inter-system conflict as incompatibility of admissible discrepancy domains + order alignment `φ_{AB}` | Conflict is not primarily semantic disagreement; it arises when interacting systems operate on non-overlapping admissible discrepancy domains, explaining why "talking past each other" persists despite shared language. | [Rep. 2026-10](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19493294), [Rep. 2026-13](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19509685) |
| **Developmental transitions and stage-like change** | Level transition as non-equivalent change in admissibility geometry + compression-induced invariant reorganization | Developmental stages are not age-dependent milestones but non-equivalent transformations of stability geometry, explaining why change appears discrete and why regression may occur under sustained overload. | [Rep. 2026-3](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19467881), [Rep. 2026-8](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19481795) |
| **Psychopathology as structural constraint** | Restricted accessibility + non-injectivity of regulatory accessibility + structural blindness | Psychopathological patterns are not merely "distorted beliefs" but consequences of admissibility constraints that exclude certain discrepancies from regulation, making structural incoherence regulatorily invisible. | [Rep. 2026-12](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19508182), [Rep. 2026-11](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19499593) |
