
Organizational / Institutional Modeling & GTCS

> *GTCS does not prescribe institutional design. It provides an architectural lens to distinguish structural constraints from political narratives, and trajectory-dependent overload from momentary mismanagement.*

| Problem / Open Question | GTCS Lens | What This Clarifies | Key References |
|-------------------------|-----------|-------------------|---------------|
| Institutional Inertia / Resistance to Reform | Overload memory `L_t` + hysteresis in admissibility boundary `T_crit(L)` | Inertia is not merely political or cultural; it is a trajectory-dependent regulatory state where accumulated non-compensated burden shifts the admissibility threshold, making structural change inadmissible until overload decays or boundary deforms. | [Rep. 2026-4](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19467913), [Rep. 2026-5](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19468033) |
| Agenda-Setting & Information Filtering | Pre-symbolic admissibility `Π^{ps}_t` + admissible discrepancy domain `D_t` | Agenda formation is a structural filter that admits only discrepancies compatible with current institutional invariants. Critical signals may exist structurally but remain regulatorily inaccessible, explaining why "obvious" issues fail to reach decision layers. | [Rep. 2026-11](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19499593), [Rep. 2026-12](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19508182) |
| KPIs, Dashboards, and Institutional "Health" Metrics | Coherence representation `Ĉ_t` as order-preserving proxy + restricted accessibility | Performance metrics are not objective mirrors of institutional state; they are compressed regulatory variables constructed from admitted signals. They preserve order under current filters but cannot reconstruct full structural deviation or detect filtered-out discrepancies. | [Rep. 2026-9](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19488084), [Rep. 2026-12](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19508182) |
| Path Dependence in Institutional Development | Trajectory-dependent regulation + non-reducibility of `L_t` to instantaneous tension | Institutional state is defined by `(T_t, L_t)`, not current conditions alone. Historical accumulation of unresolved pressures creates divergent admissibility geometries, explaining divergent responses to identical shocks without invoking exogenous cultural variables. | [Rep. 2026-4](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19467913), [Rep. 2026-5](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19468033) |
| Policy Simplification vs. Genuine Reform | Structural compression vs. simplification criterion (`E[u_{t+1}] < E[u_t]`) | Reducing rules or merging units (simplification) does not guarantee reduced institutional overload. Genuine reform (compression) must reorganize constraints to lower long-run coordination costs and preserve functional admissibility. | [Rep. 2026-8](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19481795) |
| Institutional Culture as a Stable Operational Regime | Identity-core as regulatory attractor + concentration near minimal overload | Culture is not a shared belief system but a structurally sustained low-overload region. It persists because trajectories concentrate there to minimize regulatory pressure, not because of normative commitment, socialization, or leadership alignment. | [Rep. 2026-6](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19476667) |
| Inter-Agency Coordination Failures | Inter-system conflict as incompatibility of admissible discrepancy domains `D_t` | Coordination failure is not primarily semantic or goal-based; it arises when institutions operate on non-overlapping admissible discrepancy domains. Alignment requires order preservation of regulatory variables, not just shared objectives or data exchange. | [Rep. 2026-10](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19493294), [Rep. 2026-13](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19509685) |
| Crisis Response and "Structural Blindness" | Restricted accessibility + non-injectivity of regulatory signals | Crises may be geometrically present (high deviation from stability) while remaining regulatorily inaccessible. The institution is not "ignoring" the crisis; the discrepancies defining it are filtered out by pre-symbolic admissibility constraints. | [Rep. 2026-11](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19499593), [Rep. 2026-12](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19508182) |
| Regulatory Capture and Institutional Drift | Propagation bias from repeated admissible filtering + compression-induced invariant formation | Drift is not necessarily corruption; it emerges from sustained selective admission of certain discrepancy classes, which stabilizes new proto-structural channels and eventually compresses into new invariants that reshape admissibility geometry. | [Rep. 2026-11](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19499593), [Rep. 2026-8](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19481795) |
| Incremental Adaptation vs. Paradigm Shift | Level-preserving vs. level-forming compression + non-equivalent change in admissibility geometry | Paradigm shift is not a matter of degree or intensity; it occurs only when structural transformation non-equivalently deforms the stability geometry `U(S_t)`, changing the fundamental space of admissible configurations. | [Rep. 2026-3](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19467881), [Rep. 2026-8](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19481795) |
