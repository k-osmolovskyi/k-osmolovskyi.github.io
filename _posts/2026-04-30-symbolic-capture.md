Why do humans — or, more broadly, cognitive systems — need sensations and emotions? We have already examined this question. But why should these feelings be shaped into symbols?

People often find it difficult to describe what they feel. This is where the familiar phrase comes from: “I cannot put into words what I feel.” Why does this happen?

And why is a symbolic description of feelings needed at all? After all, it may seem that one can simply feel, without reducing this entire spectrum to words and symbols.

But perhaps, if an emotional manifestation does not receive a symbolic form, the system risks failing to classify an evident problem in the future. For example, a cognitive system at the level of a state, after experiencing a strong “emotional surge,” must give it a symbolic definition. Otherwise, when a similar structure of the situation appears again, it may fail to recognize this marker in time and respond correctly.

Perhaps a cognitive system that classifies its states more broadly and more precisely in relation to emotions will respond more effectively to states that are dangerous for its structure. Or, conversely, it may become better at supporting configurations of processing in which its structure becomes stabilized.

The more precisely a cognitive system explains its own states, the more effective its further regulation becomes. This is especially true for inter-system regulation.

But there is another side to this. Perhaps limiting the manifestation of a system’s state through symbolism or expressed emotions makes the system less vulnerable in some aspects of interaction between cognitive systems. Sometimes it is more advantageous to “show less” of one’s state, in order not to reveal weakness. But in order to restrain such a manifestation, the system must itself recognize the symbols and meanings of these emotions. It must understand that this state should not be fully manifested.

And conversely, symbolic manifestation can sometimes be amplified, or even exaggerated, in order to embellish one’s state.

These questions and claims are formulated in my recently published manuscript: [Symbolic Capture of Manifestation](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.19895497)
