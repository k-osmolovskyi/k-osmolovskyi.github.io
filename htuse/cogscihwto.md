# Theoretical Cognitive Science

| Problem / Open Question | GTCS Lens | What This Clarifies |
|-------------------------|-----------|-------------------|
| **Stability–Plasticity Paradox** | Layered admissibility + joint regulatory state `(T, L)` + structural compression | Separates processing within a structure from structural updating. Plasticity is not error-driven but gated by accumulated overload and meta-accessibility, explaining why high tension sometimes yields adaptation and sometimes rigidity. |
| **Nature of Cognitive Identity** | Identity as regulatory attractor + low-overload concentration regions | Derives persistence from bounded regulation and trajectory concentration, not from self-models or narratives. Explains why identity survives content change and why it resists updating outside low-overload basins. |
| **Local Signals vs. Global State Assessment** | Geometric coherence `C_I(x_t)` vs. structural tension `T_t` vs. overload `u_t` | Distinguishes global structural deviation from local regulatory burden. Explains why systems can exhibit low local conflict while being globally incoherent, and vice versa. |
| **Partial Observability in Self-Regulation** | Restricted accessibility + coherence representation as order-preserving proxy | Shows that self-regulation does not require full state access. Representation is a compressed, regulatorily sufficient ordering over admitted discrepancies, not a reconstruction of global geometry. |
| **Cognitive Development / Ontogenesis** | Architectural levels as equivalence classes of stability geometry + level-forming compression | Defines development as trajectory through non-equivalent stability geometries, not as stage accumulation or complexity increase. Explains discrete transitions via compression-induced geometry change. |
| **Structural Blindness / Meta-Ignorance** | Pre-symbolic admissibility filtering + non-injectivity of regulatory accessibility | Explains why systems can be globally incoherent yet regulatorily "blind" to it. Inaccessibility is structural (gated by `Π^{ps}_t`), not due to noise, inattention, or failed metacognition. |
| **Status and Limits of Representation** | Representation as derived ordering over restricted admissible domain | Clarifies that representation is not a mirror of structure or reality. It is inherently partial, lossy, and regulatorily oriented, explaining why representations guide action without guaranteeing structural transparency. |
| **Cognitive Limits, Burnout, and Rigidity** | Trajectory-dependent overload memory `L_t` + compensability threshold `θ` | Distinguishes instantaneous tension from accumulated overload. Explains delayed breakdown, cognitive rigidity, and hysteresis as path-dependent accumulation, not momentary capacity exhaustion. |
| **Conflict vs. Structural Incompatibility** | Invariant interaction architecture + global conflict functional `K_t` | Reframes conflict as architectural incompatibility among historically constituted constraints, not semantic contradiction or prediction error. Explains why some conflicts are irresolvable within the current level. |
| **Role of "Error" in Cognitive Change** | Admissibility operator `A(T, L, ...)` ≠ error threshold | Structural change is not triggered by error magnitude alone. It requires sustained overload, meta-accessibility, and compression efficiency. Explains divergence between high-error adaptation and high-error collapse. |
